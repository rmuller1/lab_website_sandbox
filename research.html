What we work on

Three intertwined projects, one underlying question: how does a cell's translational state constrain what it can become?

Ribosome profiling
CLIP-seq

Selective translation in progenitors

In dividing neural progenitors, steady-state mRNA levels explain only a modest fraction of variance in protein abundance. The usual explanations — degradation rate, ribosome occupancy, sequestration — are not mutually exclusive, and existing data cannot cleanly distinguish them.

We combine ribosome profiling with RBP crosslinking in staged embryos to identify transcripts whose translational efficiency changes independently of abundance, then test whether the responsible RBP is necessary and sufficient using targeted degradation.

Light-sheet imaging
Zebrafish

Timing of fate commitment

Whether commitment is a discrete threshold crossing or a gradual loss of potency is an old question that improved imaging can finally address at the level of individual cells rather than populations.

We track single progenitors from their final symmetric division through differentiation, recording a translational reporter alongside a lineage marker, and ask when a cell's future first becomes predictable from its current state.

Optogenetics
Human organoids

Perturbing condensates on demand

Correlations between granule formation and translational repression are abundant; causal tests are rare, because most perturbations act on timescales far slower than the process itself.

Light-inducible oligomerisation lets us nucleate or dissolve RNP condensates within seconds, so that the translational response can be read out before secondary effects accumulate.
